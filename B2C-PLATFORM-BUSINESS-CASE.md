## A · Strategic framing (the one page before the slides)

**1 · Strategic thesis.**
drivEV's B2B leasing base is proven but lead flow is thinning. B2C is the next S-curve — but a cold, asset-heavy, price-war entry against Flux is how SOCAR died. The win is a **capital-light B2C platform**: partners fund the vehicles, drivEV runs the platform and owns the customer, and the money is made not on a thin platform fee but on the **stacked ecosystem margin** (platform fee + chargEV + insurance/NCD + second-life remarketing). Ramp it through the **B2B → salary-sacrifice bridge**, not cold CAC.

**2 · The Board question.**
*Should drivEV commit ~RM2–4m of one-off investment to launch an asset-light B2C platform, and under what conditions does it pay back inside ~24 months?*

**3 · Central concept.**
**"Own the customer, not the car."** drivEV becomes the platform and brand; hosts own the asset and the asset risk; the ecosystem (chargEV, insurance, second-life) is where margin stacks.

**4 · Audience & decision required.**
Primary: **CY** (pre-Board alignment), then **Louisa**, then a 95%-FPSO **Board** that needs pictures, concepts and worst/base/upside numbers — not jargon. Decision: approve a funded **POC** (platform build + PR launch + war chest + 5–6 host partners) with hard go/no-go gates.

**5 · Storyline (SCR).**
*Situation:* B2B works, leads thinning, Flux owns B2C on price with no retention. *Complication:* cold B2C is a cash-burning price war (SOCAR) and asset-light platform-fee economics alone **don't** pay back in 24 months. *Resolution:* a lean, asset-light platform that ramps via the B2B bridge and stacks ecosystem margin **does** — and de-risks the balance sheet.

**6 · Visual direction.**
drivEV interim green system (BRAND.md): Inter + IBM Plex Mono for figures, line icons (no emoji), one accent (green), amber/red reserved for risk. Board-simple: one idea per slide, a number or a picture carries each.

### Slide 5 — The asset-light model (how it works)
- **Title:** Own the customer, not the car — partners fund the asset, drivEV runs the platform
- **Key message:** Capital-light: hosts carry the vehicle, road-tax, insurance, maintenance and RV risk; drivEV collects payment, keeps a platform fee, and owns the customer.
- **Copy:** drivEV collects the subscriber payment, retains a platform fee, remits host earnings; the host bears the cost of ownership and residual-value risk. 5–6 launch partners × 30–50 vehicles each. This is what lets us scale 1,000 units without parking RM150–300m on YGT's balance sheet.
- **Visual:** Flow diagram: Subscriber → drivEV platform (fee) → Host (asset + costs + RV); side-note ecosystem margins (charging/insurance/remarketing) flowing to drivEV.
- **Data required:** **the make-or-break input** — partner-funded monthly price vs Flux's lease price for the same model.
- **Speaker notes:** The whole case rests on partner-funded price being *more* competitive than Flux. If it is, it's a no-brainer (Kelvin's words).
- **Likely questions:** Why will customers switch from Flux? → cheaper (partner-funded) + chargEV bundle + retention; needs the price proof.
- **Appendix:** Host contract terms; payment flow.
